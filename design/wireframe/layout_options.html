Mugello 2006: layout options

Same data, palette, fonts and story text as the portrait wireframe (option A). Both options here are landscape 100 × 70 cm, which gives the map more room and more air. Map in TV orientation.

Option B: map + vertical race column

How it reads: the map on the left says where. On the right, time runs top to bottom: a vertical position chart (1st on the left) with the 7 story cards beside it, each linked to its lap. This is your "timeline next to the map", using a familiar chart.

Strengths: one meaning per element (map = place, column = time); the race reads like a TV timing tower. Weak spots: a vertical position chart is less common than a horizontal one, and the cards are denser than in A.

Option C: the race as a ring around Mugello

How it reads: your idea. The whole race is wrapped once around the circuit, clockwise from the start line. Each line is a rider, and the closer to the track, the higher the position. The story cards run in a vertical column on the right.

Strengths: striking, unique, and Mugello's shape carries everything. Weak spots: the lanes can't follow the exact outline (it folds back on itself in the infield, so they'd cross the track), so they follow a rounded silhouette instead. A spot on the ring means a time, not a place, so lap 15 sits near Bucine, not San Donato. It needs the "how to read" note.
