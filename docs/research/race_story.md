# Mugello 2006: the race story

**Gran Premio d'Italia Alice, Mugello, Sunday 4 June 2006.** 23 laps × 5,245 m = 120.635 km.
Dry: air 20°, track 29°, 14% humidity. Start at 14:01:24.

### Rossi vs Capirossi, lap by lap
(The gap is Capirossi's total race time minus Rossi's at the line; negative means Capirossi is ahead.)

1. **Laps 1–14: Rossi in control, Capirossi hunting.** Rossi leads 11 of the first 14 laps;
   Gibernau leads laps 2–4. Capirossi falls from the front row to **8th after lap 1**
   and then moves through the field: 8th → 7th (lap 5) → 5th (lap 9) → 4th (lap 11) → 3rd (lap 13).
   The gap shrinks from 2.2 s (lap 10: 1.95 s) to **0.157 s** on lap 14.
2. **Lap 15: the turn.** Rossi's lap is 1'52.438 (his slowest racing lap). Almost all the time
   is lost in sector 1, 27.086 s against his usual ~25.9–26.0 s.
   He drops **from 1st to 5th** and Capirossi takes the lead. *What happened there needs a
   source (a mistake? running wide? a pass gone wrong?).*
3. **Laps 15–22: the comeback.** Rossi climbs 5th → 4th → 3rd (lap 17, with a 1'50.442,
   the fastest lap anyone set from lap 14 on) → 2nd (lap 18) and sits on Capirossi's tail.
   The gap at the line: −0.124, −0.142, −0.036, −0.082, then **−0.002 s on lap 22**.
4. **Lap 23: the last lap.** Rossi passes and wins by **0.575 s**. Hayden is only 0.16 s
   further back.

The lap chart shows **3 different leaders at the line** (Rossi, Gibernau, Capirossi), with
Hayden, Pedrosa and Melandri all in the top 4 at some point. That's the "epic" in the
podcast's description.

## Context from the Paddock Pass Podcast (20th-anniversary episode)

Source: *"Why Valentino Rossi's 2006 MotoGP Mugello success still ranks as one of his best"*,
Paddock Pass Podcast (Adam Wheeler, David Emmett, Neil Morrison), May 2026, about
[17:00–50:00] of the video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PFMQWNjPgQM.
(Episode page: https://paddockpasspodcast.com/episodes/558/paddock-pass-podcast-episode-558-mugello-memories-was-2006-valentino-rossis-finest)

These notes come from YouTube's automatic captions, so the quotes are lightly cleaned up.
These are the hosts' claims, **not verified against the data** unless stated.

**Rossi's back against the wall**
- Rossi arrived **43 points behind** in the championship with **only one win** so far
  in 2006. Neil called it "the first time … since he had stepped up into the premier class …
  when he had his back properly pressed up against the wall" [20–21m, 46m].
- Why: he'd flirted with F1 (Ferrari tests) over the winter. Michelin's new 2006 tyres
  made the Yamaha M1 "chatter horrendously", and his engine blew at the previous race, Le
  Mans. His season started with Elías knocking him down at the first corner of round 1 at
  Jerez [22–23m, 51m].
- Hayden came in **leading the championship**, narrowly ahead of Melandri and Capirossi,
  with podiums at all of the first four races [35–36m].

**The era**
- This was the **last year of the 990cc** bikes and the height of the **Michelin vs
  Bridgestone tyre war**. Capirossi (Ducati, on Bridgestones) won the season opener at Jerez.
  Michelin's factory riders got tailor-made "Saturday night special" tyres [21m, 24–27m].
- Ducati raced a **special livery for its 80th anniversary** (green, silver and red).
  Capirossi was 33 and rode in huge padded gloves after earlier hand injuries [28m, 31m, 44m].
- Top speed then was 333 km/h (Capirossi, which matches our data: 333.5) against 366 km/h in
  the modern era [38m].

**How the race unfolded**
- Seven contenders early on: Gibernau, Rossi, Melandri, Hayden, Pedrosa, Stoner, Capirossi
  [34m]. Gibernau's pole would be the **last of his career** [31m].
- **Stoner** made a brilliant start up to P4, then crashed at **Arrabbiata 2**, with the Honda
  "flipping end over end" [33–34m]. *(Data: out after lap 8.)*
- **Capirossi** got a poor start and was "over 3 seconds back" early, then set
  "mad crazy fastest lap times to draw himself right into contention" [33m, 35m].
  *(Data: 8th after lap 1, 2.2 s behind at the line, fastest lap on lap 7.)*
- **Gibernau** reportedly had a problem with his left foot/slider, which he used to judge
  lean angle, and it cost him confidence for a few laps. "Had that not happened, you could
  have been looking at … a Sete Gibernau win" [40m]. *(The captions are unclear here and it
  may be Melandri who ran off track; verify.)*
- **Rossi** ran the **softer front tyre** and had to nurse it. He followed Gibernau early and
  waited: "Patience is the most underrated … virtue of a motorcycle racer" (Emmett) [41–42m].
- **Lap 15:** "he was leading and then got dropped down to fifth in the space of like three
  or four corners" [40–41m]. *(The data confirms 1st → 5th; the cause is still unknown.)*
- **The finish:** "It was almost as if he was waiting for Capirossi … He knew that Capirossi
  was going to come." Rossi then set a strong lap "on the final lap to just keep him
  behind" (Morrison) [46m]. *(Data: Rossi's last lap was 1'50.877 against Capirossi's 1'51.454.)*
- "Capirossi I think was a couple of laps away from this being his best ever Grand Prix"
  [46m].
- Hayden "was just chipping away in the background". It was his **only podium at
  Mugello** [34m, 44m].

**After the flag: the emotion**
- "Not theatrical … more just raw emotion", which showed "he felt he had done something maybe
  even a little bit unexpected" [46–47m]. He parked the bike and swaggered over to the fans.
- A **track invasion on the cool-down lap**: "so many people on the track"; the crowd around
  the podium on the main straight was "a huge Rossi festival" [42–43m].
- On the podium Capirossi was "the fatherly good guy" [44m].
- It was Rossi's **5th Mugello win in the premier class, with two more to come** [43m]. Neil
  ranks it **among Rossi's top five victories** with Barcelona 2009, Laguna Seca 2008, Phillip
  Island 2003 and Welkom 2004 [45m].
- Aftermath: Rossi won again at Barcelona, then broke his wrist at Assen and retired with a
  technical problem at Laguna Seca, and the season went down to Valencia 2006 [49–51m].
